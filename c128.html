C128

Claims: 7 Part of C

The C128 is of interest to those who cut their teeth on early PCs. 1 The C128 has separate chips for 40-column and 80-column video outputs, with the 80-column output requiring a different cable and not being used for most software. 2 The 80-column output requires three adapters to connect to a normal monitor, but the C128 can run in 80-column mode with an OSSC. 34 GEOS runs on the C128, and it can boot directly into a fully loaded desktop in about 12 seconds by holding control at startup. 56 The C128 combined C64 compatibility, CP/M compatibility, and a more capable native mode including 80-column text, along with partial PET/VIC20 compatibility. 7
