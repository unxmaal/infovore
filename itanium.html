Itanium

Claims: 201 Background: 19 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see Itanium, Gentoo Linux, LLVM, MIPS architecture processors, XFS.

With HP

The development of Itanium started at HP before Intel joined, and the effort was primarily aimed at replacing PA-RISC. 12 HP's Itanium effort did not fare well and survived only due to financial backing. 3 In ~2008, HP salespeople reported still having financial industry customers on VMS/VAX supported on custom contracts, while Itanium was met with ambivalence by customers who had moved on or weren't bothering. 4 HP has not sold a custom compiler for Itanium in some time, and there is no 64-bit version of GCC for Itanium on HP-UX. 56 The compiler for Itanium on HP-UX can only produce 32-bit code, and HP-UX has 32-bit ELFs on Itanium. 78 The book about Itanium is written by a lead architect for Itanium and a director of marketing at HP. 9

General

Itanium was a 64-bit CPU architecture released by Intel in June 2001, designed differently from x86 and incompatible with it in hardware. 10111213 It was a VLIW architecture with over 100 general registers, but it never had a consumer variant and was never widely adopted. 141516 Poor software support and poor performance from the start meant nobody invested in compiler optimization, and it was eventually outcompeted by AMD64. 1718 There were hardware issues with Itanium, including poor x86 emulation and bus bottlenecks. 1920 Itanium could run Windows and Solaris, and it is still used for legacy workloads. 212223 Itanium was a processor consortium that could run HP-UX, VMS, Linux, and Windows, though it was not x86. 242526 The platform struggled with poor performance, compiler issues, and difficulty running x86 software. 2728 Itanium chips had a good microarchitecture, but building VLIW compilers was difficult, making compiled C often worse than on other platforms. 2926 Nobody is working on VLIW compilers anymore because Itanium failed in the market. 30 Gentoo is the only OS still maintained for Itanium. 31 Itanium machines are disliked by their owners. 3233 Itanium was a reference design not unique to Intel or HP, and was criticized for poor performance with IA-32 applications, which were added late in the design process and performed poorly. 34353637 The original Itanium struggled with IA-32 code performance, and IA-32 support on the native IA-64 OS was added late. 363738 Itanium was a commercial failure associated with commercial failures, with development costs running into billions and poor management leading to its failure in the market. 394041 Itanium was seen as beneficial for the UNIX market to unify vendors and possibly compete with Wintel, but Linux distributions dropped Itanium support, though users can still run Itanium with older distros or self-built software. 4243 Later Itanium CPUs were better, though not without challenges, and the processors were still being supported in 2020 despite manufacturing continuing until at least that year. 4445 The Itanium was a high-performance processor that failed to gain widespread adoption despite initial support from multiple companies, and it was too late, too big, and too expensive, getting outperformed in the MHz race by cheap x86. 4647 It was designed as a better accelerator but failed as a general-purpose processor, and it was obsolete by the time it was released. 4849 Itanium was primarily used in high-performance computing and databases, with only a few workstations supporting it, mainly the HP zx2000 and zx6000. 50 There were a small number of unique Itanium designs, with rebrands from various companies. 51 The Itanium had a brief edge of about 20% in pure FP performance but didn't last. 52 Itanium processors have SIMD capabilities, though not as excellent as expected. 53 The Itanium is not energy efficient, consuming a lot of power and heating up a room. 54 There is no interest in using Itanium, but keeping the current setup is preferred. 55 The Itanium can achieve up to 4.7 MIPS on an rx2800i2, with an average of 4.0 MIPS. 56 The LLVM backend for Itanium has no target-specific optimizations and handles boolean operations using control flow, which may explain the performance issues. 57 These processors put threads to sleep when waiting for memory access, making them worse than Itanium for emulation. 58 Itanium versions of SDRC I-DEAS, Patran, and Nastran were available on Linux or HP-UX. 59
