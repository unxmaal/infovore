Indigo

Claims: 379 Background: 10 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI Indigo, SGI O2.

With Indy

The Indigo and Indy share several hardware features, including EISA expansion slots, Floptical drives, and 8-bit graphics options, though the Indigo had proper accelerated 3D graphics while the Indy lacked them until the XZ. 123 Both machines use standard 72-pin FPM parity SIMMs, and the Indigo stores its MAC address in an EEPROM on the backplane while the Indy uses a Dallas chip. 45 The R5k in an Indy is faster than an Indigo unless doing 3D, and the XZ graphics in the Indy differs from the XZ in the Indigo and Indigo2. 67 SCSI2SD adapters work well with both systems, and a deep working knowledge of every subsystem is required for the Indy and Indigo. 89

With Indigo2

The SGI Indigo and Indigo2 are MIPS-based workstations with 3D graphics capabilities, and the Indigo with Elan Graphics is superior to an IRIS Indigo. 101112 The Indigo2 uses standard PS2 keyboards and mice, whereas the Indigo requires a specific custom keyboard and mouse that can damage PS2 devices. 131415 The system case design for SGI's MIPS-using machines up to the Indigo was influenced by streamline moderne. 16 The difference between 60 and 70 ns RAM in an Indigo or Indigo2 does not significantly impact performance. 17 8-bit graphics on the Indigo and Indigo2 can cause color distortion when opening multiple graphics demos. 18 The PROM firmware of Indigo and Indigo2 can be configured for little-endian operation, but this requires custom firmware and the engineering skills to build it. 19 The Indigo is not an i2. 20

performance with texture-mapped polygons. 79 The SGI Indigo is a desktop version of the IRIS, released in 1991 and based on miniaturized Personal IRIS hardware. 8081 It is essentially a stack of three
