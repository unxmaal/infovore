Indigo2

Claims: 959 Background: 46 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI Indigo2, IRIX, Power supply unit (computer), SGI Indigo, SGI Indy, Silicon Graphics, XFS.

With Indy

The Indigo2 and Indy are incompatible when left alone together, though the Indigo2 works where the Indy does not. 123 The Indigo2 is lighter than the Indy, which is larger. 45 The Indigo2 and Indy share specifications, including RAM, motherboard, and graphics chips, and both offer XL graphics as a low-end option with the same chips but different board layouts. 678 The Indigo2 and Indy use SD cards for emulated SCSI, offering faster random access, and both accept PS/2 keyboards and mice. 910 The Indy has two IO card slots, while the Indigo2 has four due to the graphics and mezzanine board occupying the bottom slots. 11 The XZ in the Indy is different from the XZ in the Indigo2, and both were designed for monitor-on-top. 1213 The Indigo2 and Indy may suffer from severe PSU capacitor leakage, and the Indy PSU can be problematic. 1415 The Indigo2 and Indy run the same software and can run up to IRIX 6.5.22, with the Indigo2 being more compact and lighter than the Indy. 16171819 The Indigo2 loads programs about five to ten times faster than the Indy. 20 The Indy and Indigo2 became the O2 and Octane, respectively. 21 The Indy and Indigo2 can run IRIX and provide a full desktop experience, and can run up to IRIX 6.5.22. 1822 The Indy and Indigo2 are good choices for running MAME, and MAME can emulate an Indy or Indigo2 with XL graphics and networking for IRIX 5.3 to 6.5. 2324 The Indy and Indigo2 use standard 72 pin FPM parity SIMMs, and the R4K Indigo, Indigo2, and Indy all share the same 72-pin FPM RAM. 2526 The Indy and Indigo2 could not capture or compress video in real time, and the Cosmo Compress is a hardware MJPEG engine. 27 IndyVideo+CosmoCompress is an available video compression board for an Indy and an IMPACT Compression board is for an Indigo2. 28

With O2

The O2 and Indigo2 are vintage SGI machines, with the O2 taking an R5k CPU and the Indigo2 running an R10k CPU. 2930 The O2 was designed as a low-budget computer compared to the Indigo2, which was seen as top-of-the-line when it came out. 31 The O2 and Indigo2 both have presenter cards, and both can run MAME, CATIA v4, and Flame 8.34. 323334 The O2 and Indigo2 support hardware texture mapping and hardware transform and lighting, features not available on earlier models or on PC graphics until 1999. 35 The O2 and Indigo2 may fail with too many Ethernet interrupts, and their system ID coin cells are Dallas one-wire devices that store the base MAC address and lmhostid. 3637 The system ID coin cell is on the PCI riser of the O2 and on the NIC module in the back of the system board of the Indigo2. 38 The O2 and Indigo2 are among the most affordable SGI machines, with Indigo2 systems often available for under $300 and O2s for $100-$200. 3940 The Indigo2 and O2 are top picks for some users, and the Indigo2 appears in a Star Wars Episode I documentary, with ILM primarily using Indigo2s initially before switching to O2s later. 4142

With R10000

The Indigo2 R10000 lineup includes a low-end option without SolidImpact graphics, and the Impact 10000, which uses the R10k as the fastest CPU in the Indigo2 lineup. 4344 The Indigo2 can scale up to 1GB of RAM with R10k CPUs, though official support is 768 MB with 64 MB sticks, while 1GB is supported unofficially using specific RAM modules. 4546 An r10k/impact Indigo2 needs IRIX 6.2 or higher, though IRIX 6.2 did not run on Indigo2 R10K initially and a later version was required. 4748 The Indigo2 can support a four-card video stack including an IMPACT Channel Option card, and the Max IMPACT graphics perform well but may feel stuttery on older hardware like a 195 MHz R10K. 49 Upgrading an Indigo2 from an R4400 CPU to an R10000 requires a motherboard change, and upgrading the motherboard to an r10k and PSU is required, though Impact units may not need a PSU upgrade. 5051 Some Indigo2 Impact models with R10k badges actually had R4k/250 CPUs inside, so checking the sticker in the back is necessary. 52

With Octane

An Indigo2 at max spec is effectively a low-end Octane with better expandability and is stackable. 53 The fastest Indigo2 is comparable in performance to the slowest Octane. 54 The Octane2 is heavier than the Indigo2 but offers RGBA 48-bit color and dual CPU capabilities. 55 The Octane is more fragile than the Indigo2. 56 The Indigo2 PSU is less reliable than the Octane's. 57 The Indigo2 and Octane require different system bus chips, so the HQ needs to be connected accordingly. 58 The Indigo2 and Octane were popular during the heyday of SGI. 59

With PSU

The Indigo2 PSU is used for Indigo2 systems and comes in different types, requiring 3.5V rails. 6061 It is mechanically complex and difficult to repair, especially when recapped, which is rated 9 out of 10 for difficulty and can cause damage if delayed. 6263 The PSU has issues with MOSFETs breaking and capacitors drying out, with the 330uf 6.3V red caps being common culprits, and its stamped and bent sheet metal design makes repairs challenging. 646566 It can fail and lock up the machine, output high frequency noise during data transfer, and produce heat even when the machine is off. 67686970 It can handle extreme heat and still function, and a failure can be fixed by replacing it with a working unit from another Indigo2. 7172 Fitting an ATX PSU is challenging due to size and voltage requirements, and Indigo2 systems are generally reliable, especially the teal model, but may need PSU servicing. 6073

With IRIX 5.3

The Indigo2 can run IRIX 5.3, though it is not on the supported OS list and may require a CPU swap for 5.3. 7475 IRIX 5.3 requires a specific version for Indigo2 with 2 Mb cache. 76 The Indigo2 runs IRIX 5.3 and 6.5.22, but it cannot run Git. 77 FreeGLUT 3.4.0 compiles and runs demos on IRIX 5.3, though some require newer OpenGL versions not supported by Indigo2. 78

With IRIX

The Indigo2 is a CPU/processor type system that runs IRIX or IRIX64, which can be checked with `uname`. 7980 The Indigo2 motherboard supports R4K CPUs and requires at least 160MB RAM to run IRIX, with XZ/Extreme graphics recommended. 81 The Indigo2 should be able to run IRIX ports, depending on graphics and RAM amount. 82 The Indigo2 PROM does not understand the G160 card, but it understands the 3com EISA card, which requires Phobos drivers for G160 functionality under IRIX. 83 Network install of IRIX on an SGI Indigo 2 is possible using NFS. 84 The Indigo2 installation process involves booting with specific commands, repartitioning, resizing, labeling, syncing, and installing the IRIX system software from a Debian virtual machine. 85

With Indigo

The SGI Indigo and Indigo2 are MIPS-based workstations with 3D graphics capabilities, and the Indigo with Elan Graphics is superior to a standard IRIS Indigo. 868788 The two machines are distinct: the Indigo2 uses standard PS2 keyboards and mice, while the Indigo requires a custom keyboard and mouse that can damage PS2 devices. 899091 The Indigo and Indigo2 keyboards differ in cable design, and the difference between 60 and 70 ns RAM does not significantly impact performance. 9293 The PROM firmware of both machines can be configured for little-endian operation, but this requires custom firmware and the engineering skills to build it. 94 8-bit graphics on the Indigo and Indigo2 can cause color distortion when opening multiple graphics demos. 95

With R4000

The Indigo2 was launched during the IRIX 4.x era, and 4.x runs on R3k and R4k Indigo1 and early R4k Indigo2 models. 96 Indigo2 models have CPUs ranging from 100 MHz to 250 MHz R4000 or R4400, 75 MHz R8000, or 175/195 MHz R10000. 97 The Indigo2 R4000 runs IRIX 6.5.22 and registers as 'XZ' in HINV when equipped with extreme graphics. 9899 An Indigo2 with a 100 MHz R4000 is faster than the user-24f0 IRIS with a 36 MHz R3000. 100 Maya 4.5 is the newest version that runs on R4K Indigo2 and can render at usable resolution; versions 5.0 and up require too much RAM. 101 The Indigo2 can be upgraded with new CPU cards, and an idea for a higher-binned R4k or R5k part could enable a 400 MHz R5k. 102

With Impact

The Indigo2 IMPACT is a variant of the Indigo2 that uses IMPACT graphics cards, which can be installed in place of the GR3-Elan card but requires reinstallation of the graphics subsystems for the card to function. 103104 Indigo2 Impact motherboards come with factory-applied patches held in place with clear glue, and the dual 75MHz IP21 boards do not work with IR graphics or IMPACT. 105106 Installing IRIX 5.3 with IMPACT via LOVE on an Indigo2 requires a ZuluSCSI kit. 107 The Indigo2 Impact may have TRAM, though the specific model is unclear. 108

With ZuluSCSI

The ZuluSCSI kit is useful for drive imaging on the Indigo2, and it works fine there without requiring a config file. 109110111 However, the ZuluSCSI may require special configuration or be difficult to get working on an Indigo2. 112 It can give SCSI interface parity errors when using the default settings, and backing off to SCSI1 and enabling parity does not resolve the issue. 113 The ZuluSCSI on an Indigo2 is slow due to compatibility settings and may not benefit from BlueSCSI V2 without hardware changes. 114 ZuluSCSI and SCSI2SD are acceptable for an Indigo2, with faster access times even if spinning rust may have more throughput for larger transfers. 115
