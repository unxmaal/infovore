VLB

Claims: 14 Background: 1 claim already covered by Wikipedia, see Industry Standard Architecture.

The 486 board with ISA and VLB can start and enter BIOS but fails to boot from floppy or harddisk, stalling or printing 'press any key to reboot'. 1 ISA and VLB are compatible bus types. 2 The disk controller in the 486 uses the VLB and can cache drives using some system RAM. 3 Most motherboards had only one VLB slot, making multiple VLB slots rare, unsupported, and prone to bus crashes. 4 LB runs at the bus speed, potentially outperforming PCI in systems with multiple VLB slots. 5 LB and Fast Ethernet coexisted with PCI and EISA, with LB being the low-end option. 6 The MIPS NT deskstation Tyne uses LB and lacks EISA, and the Mips Mangum uses either ISA or EISA. 7 The PS/2 76i/77i is a LB and MCA machine that works well with a system partition and a configuration partition. 8
