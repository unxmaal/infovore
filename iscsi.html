iSCSI

Claims: 15 Background: 1 claim already covered by Wikipedia, see SCSI.

iSCSI is a SCSI transport option that can run over Ethernet, dial-up, ham radio, FDDI, and Daynaport, though some of these combinations are rare or require tuning. 12345 SCSI can be carried over FC, SAS, iSCSI, or FireWire. 6 Fibre Channel has lower latency and is more reliable than iSCSI for storage applications, while iSCSI over Ethernet can be faster and uses standard networking hardware. 1 iSCSI and FCoE can use regular Ethernet, whereas InfiniBand is more expensive and requires TCP/IP or iSER/SRB configuration, and cannot boot x86 servers. 7 NVMeOF is a newer storage protocol that can run over FC or RDMA networks, offering lower latency than iSCSI. 8 iSCSI is used for storage in various contexts, including FreeNAS, ZFS for VMs over InfiniBand, Proxmox shared storage and VM migrations, and booting and rooting systems such as the U45 over 10Gbit Ethernet. 9101112 iSCSI is not compatible enough with most Unix systems to be useful for vintage computing. 13 Someone has not built an Ethernet iSCSI to IDE/SATA/SAS/SCSI bridge or a PCIe to iSCSI adapter with a networked hard drive that speaks iSCSI. 14
