Intel

Claims: 47 Background: 5 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see AMD, Intel, Itanium, MacOS, Pentium.

Intel has attempted to move away from x86 architecture multiple times throughout the x86 CPU lifespan. 1 Intel developed the Itanium architecture, which began at HP before Intel joined. 23 Intel convinced DEC, SGI, and HP to abandon their own designs and join the Itanium project, which was supposed to dominate the market and kill AMD. 4 Intel's x86 engineers could not replicate specific performance cases of Itanium, leading to initial overestimation of Itanium's superiority but later realization of its limitations in general use. 5 AMD's 64-bit standard is what won at the end; Intel's Itanium failed and SGI declined. 6 Intel's x86 processors dominate the server market, accounting for over 61% of global datacenter server processors. 7 Intel's low chip cost and large volume sales model outcompeted SGI's high-margin, big-iron approach. 8 Intel outspent SGI on R&D, and SGI's MIPS acquisition was unsuccessful. 9 Compaq sold the IP off to Intel, which killed the architecture. 10 The PCI vendor ID for Intel is 0x8086. 11 The merger of Intel, HP, and SGI was problematic. 12
