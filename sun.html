With SGI

SGI and Sun were early internet adopters, with Sun, HP, and IBM connecting before SGI, and Sun pioneering network boot with the slogan 'the network is the computer' while SGI had it built in from the start. 12 SGI prioritized fast graphics even at the cost of working in only one system, while Sun aimed for compatibility and adherence to internal standards. 3 Users doing 2D visualization of massive datasets preferred Suns, while those doing 3D visualization wanted SGIs. 4 SGIs lack a deep firmware-level diagnostic like Suns, which can output diag/boot data over serial even if video or keyboard fails. 5 Sun and SGI used custom MMUs in their 68010-based workstations to achieve independent Unix functionality, and the IRIX kernel includes contributions from SGI and AT&T with some Sun code in NFS. 67 Sun hardware was similar to later SGI hardware, and Fujitsu and NEC rebadged Sun and SGI systems respectively. 89

General

Oracle acquired Sun because of its database deployments. 10 Sun's product line included the UltraSPARC processor family, which is 64-bit and features a lot of cache, though UltraSPARC CPUs can be slow. 111213 Sun systems include the u45, blade 2500, hypersparcs for a ss20, and the SPARCstation design, with some users keeping vintage SPARCstations from their workplaces. 141516 Sun systems run Solaris 2.5.1 to Solaris 10. 17 T2 processors are the newest Sun processors that don't support current Solaris 11 builds. 18 Sun made USB versions of the Type 6 and Type 7 mouse and keyboard, and Sun keyboards, especially Ultras, were generally good. 1920 Sun made a significant effort to open up Solaris. 21 Sun could open Solaris because it had licensed from AT&T rights equivalent to ownership of System V, a status not shared by other System V derivatives; the Unix copyright is now owned by Micro Focus International. 22 Sun encouraged cloning of its hardware, with many companies producing SPARC and SBus clones, and made OEM motherboards used by clone makers and some whiteboxes such as Integrix or Tatung. 232425 Solbourne made Sun-compatible clones with their own hardware and operating system patches, though Solbourne keyboards are not compatible with Sun and can cause damage if plugged in. 2627 Sun designed the ZX, Creator 3D, and Elite 3D cards, while the Expert 3D is based on a Wildcat design. 28 Sun's drivers do not expose 3D acceleration features on the PGX64, XVR-100, XVR-300, or PGX32. 29 The XVR-1000/4000 caused much agitation at Sun, and some blamed the SPARC workstation's demise on them. 30 Sun makes high-quality SCSI accessories. 31 Sun and SGI machines use 512-byte blocks regardless of the interface. 32 Sun is a Unix computer manufacturer whose boxes run SysV Unix and are cheap, available, and support multiple operating systems. 33 Sun developed the E10000 from the Cray Superserver CS6400, acquiring its basic technology from Silicon Graphics Inc in July 1996. 3435 Sun used the MicroSPARC in Java stations, and the UltraSPARC can run the system with minimal load impact. 3637 SVR4, a joint project of AT&T Unix System Laboratories and Sun Microsystems, introduced ELF, which was inherited by IRIX5 and up. 38 Sun servers used Veritas to mirror root drives for failure protection, which seemed to come with IRIX. 39 Sun provided breakout cables for Suns with only one physical port for serial A/B, and a standard DB25 -> DB9 adapter also works for serial A. 4041 Sun used AAUI on some 10 Mb NICs briefly. 42 The fastest SCSI CD-ROM drive fits in a Sun enclosure with various 68-pin cables available. 43 The SPARCstation has a TurboSPARC while the JavaStation had a MicroSPARC, both similar to the SPARCstation 5 with different details. 44 Sun's naming system was inconsistent, often adding an 'E' for speed upgrades or minor updates, such as the Ultra1, which had a different mainboard with added UPA, UW SCSI, and Fast Ethernet. 4546 The UltraSPARC core was used up to 2005, by which point it was obsolete, and it struggles with multi-CPU tasks compared to dual and quad systems. 4748 The SPARC64 CPUs are faster per clock than UltraSPARC CPUs, but Sun did not upgrade UltraSPARC systems to PCIe and DDR RAM. 49 Sun's pivot to massive SMP systems and the UltraSPARC design was not enough to compete with Intel's larger design teams and install base. 50 Sun invested in software despite Linux commoditizing hardware, leading to acquisitions and cultural challenges. 51 Sun Microsystems was a major desktop competitor alongside HP, IBM, and DEC, and its SPARCstations were used in various professional settings, including Pixar's SPARCstation farms and original PhotoCD workstations with the PREMIER retouching solution. 52535455 Sun developed multiple generations of SCSI and network cards but did not focus on SBus cards, defaulted to 8-bit PCI, and discontinued SPARC desktops around 2006. 56 The SuperSPARC CPU was a failure for Sun, with initial samples not reaching 90 MHz, while the newer hyperSPARCs were positioned as an upgrade for those who couldn't afford UltraSPARC. 5758 Sun's MicroSPARC benchmarks were inflated, and the O2 with a base CPU without L2 cache is slow and difficult to benchmark, especially with RQSALL. 5960 Sun monitors can have leaky flybacks, and Sun's serial numbers are not sequential and can't be assumed to start at 0001. 6162 Sun dongles were phased out with the transition from Sun to x86 systems, and newer Sun systems likely used USB dongles. 63
