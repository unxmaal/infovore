General

The PowerSeries is an SGI machine that took an R3K 40 MHz CPU and used a proprietary bus called PowerPath, with some VME slots for expansion cards. 12 The PowerSeries and Personal Iris use the same sleds, and the same exact PSU is used in their PowerSeries. 34 The Pro Iris and Power Series use DA15 connectors, and the PowerSeries uses a proprietary bus (PowerPath) with some VME slots for expansion cards. 52 The PowerSeries run hot and have brittle solder after 30 years, and their modular PSUs have a backplane with control logic and an aux voltage regulator that powers the fan. 67 The PowerSeries is robust, with most issues related to graphics, particularly RealityEngine and VGX boards. 8 The PowerSeries can be upgraded with an IP17 board to become an R4000-50, also known as a 4D/510. 9 The Crimson is essentially the last iteration of the PowerSeries and can be upgraded from it. 10 PowerSeries is a 4D/440 VGX that originally had one CPU board, labeled as a 4D/420. 11 Skywriter was a tall rack brown PowerSeries with versions called Predator, which could have up to 8 CPUs and one graphics pipe, while the SkyWriter had dual VGXT graphics pipes and space for 4 CPUs. 12 A PowerSeries can run on a 15A circuit at 110VAC but is safer on 20A, while a tall rack 4D/380 requires 200-240VAC and 30A. 13 The PSU for a tall rack PowerSeries, like a SkyWriter or 4D/380, has triple modules, a 5V@250A module, cannot run on 110VAC, is 'always on', and doesn't respond to the power switch. 14 The PowerSeries or Crimson uses a separate power inlet receptacle, allowing for voltage selection at the PSU via bridged terminals '1' and '2', with 230VAC indicated by opening terminals. 15 DMediaPro was used for single frame I/O on PowerSeries, Personal IRIS, and Indigo. 16 Documentation errors exist, including mixing up PowerSeries and Professional IRIS. 17
