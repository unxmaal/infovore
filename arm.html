ARM

Claims: 66 Background: 5 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see ARM architecture family, Central processing unit.

ARM is a RISC architecture that has become prevalent in mobile devices, some servers, and high-performance computing, while taking over markets with few exceptions. 123 The software gap between ARM and x86 has dropped dramatically, with other operating systems now viable on ARM and large multi-CPU data center servers existing. 45 ARM has faced issues with standardization and compatibility among different chip implementations. 6 RISC-V has changed business models for CPU companies hoping to attract users away from ARM, and ARM has made development tools and files free, possibly in response to RISC-V's free access. 78 RISC-V is optimized for low-power cases but is slower per watt and per clock cycle than ARM or AMD64, and is expected to match x86 performance and ARM energy efficiency in about ten years. 910 Some companies use ARM cores, including Fujitsu and Sony (in the Vita). 1112 ARM was designed as a good choice for scaling down big-iron CPUs, and its first implementation was a simulation on the BBC Micro running on a 6502 processor. 1314 ARM dominates the mobile industry, which is much larger than the desktop and server markets, with almost every mobile device using ARM processors. 1516 ARM powers nearly all Android devices, Apple products, some Windows products, and many servers. 17 Non-modern RISC processors like MIPS have poor code density, which is why ARM includes features like Thumb. 18 MIPS had problematic licensing and support, making it difficult to integrate into systems compared to ARM, and the reversal of the Open MIPS initiative in 2019 further affected MIPS's competitive position against ARM. 1920 RISC-V's popularity has declined, largely due to SiFive's influence, while ARM is often cheaper and easier to use with better support. 21
