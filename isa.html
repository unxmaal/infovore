ISA

Claims: 23 Background: 3 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see Industry Standard Architecture, PCI-X.

ISA is a bus interface that can support 2 or 3 parallel ports, and it can be bridged from PCI using an IDE-CF adapter or a SiL 3112 SATA controller, with DOS relying on the BIOS for these functions. 12 ISA slots and cards were standardized to an 8.33 MHz clock speed, which contributed to its longevity, and it usually runs at a divider of the CPU bus clock, often /4. 34 ISA is compatible with VLB, and EISA slots are deeper, with added address lines that do not connect for regular ISA. 56 PISA is a combined PCI+ISA slot that also works directly for ISA cards, using a connector similar to EISA for ISA compatibility. 7 ISA can theoretically deliver around 133 Mbps, even if the connection to the rest of the system is slower. 8 ISA was widely used in the P5 era, which offered good mixes of PCI and ISA slots supporting DOS, ISA, and PCI, and could run operating systems like Solaris x86, NT4, and Win2k. 9 ISA slots appear in various machines, including the Alphastation (supporting Windows NT 3.51), the Sun386i, and some HP/Compaq EVO desktop boards, though P4 boards with ISA are rare. 101112 Some ISA cards, such as the SMC Etherlink III and 3Com 3c509 network cards, were popular in the '90s, with the Etherlink III being ISA and the 3c509 being PCI. 13
