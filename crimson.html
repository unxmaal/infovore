Crimson

Claims: 128 Background: 2 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI Onyx, Silicon Graphics.

With Onyx

The Onyx is the successor to the Crimson, differing from it in CPU count and overall capability, with the Crimson having only one R4000 CPU while the Onyx can have multiple CPUs and being more advanced in every way. 12 The Onyx is faster and more integrated than the Crimson, with fewer wires, no fan noise, and it never caught fire, while the Crimson requires additional wiring and connectors between VME cards and the front bulkhead. 345 The Sun Onyx is heavier than the Sun Crimson due to its drive arrangement and more VME cards. 3 The Onyx can be upgraded with InfiniteReality or a Crimson to RealityEngine, though the Onyx is described as a reliability nightmare while the Crimson is pretty solid except for RealityEngine. 67 Crimson and Onyx systems had spots for upgrade stickers, and model codes changed with upgrades. 8 The Indigo keyboard part number works with Personal IRIS, Crimson, and Onyx, though an adapter is needed for Crimson. 9 Onyx, Crimson, and Octane are among the IRIS systems, and Onyx and Crimson systems were on display at the SGI Mobile Innovation Center. 1011

General

The Crimson is a low-end, single-CPU R4000 (100 MHz) or R4400 (100 MHz or 150 MHz) Indigo1 with a large graphics subsystem, and it can be configured with RE graphics up to an R4400 150 MHz. 12131415 It runs IRIX, boots into IRIX, and is prone to hardware failures, being difficult to maintain due to its point-to-point wiring and numerous jumpers. 161718 The Crimson is very loud with many fans, and it supports only IP17 boards. 19202122 It cannot run IRIX 6.5, is not suitable for clustering due to its single CPU limitation, and its Entry graphics are identical to those of the IRIS Indigo. 232425 Physically, it is lightweight and can be lifted by one person, has rollers, and is the same size as the Onyx1. 262728 It was a 64-bit system built to reach market and supports MIPS-II binaries on its R4K architecture. 2930 The Crimson offers three CPU speeds—100 MHz, 133 MHz, and 150 MHz—with the 133 MHz being a modified 150 MHz CPU installed in a 100 MHz board. 31 It uses a regular socket and powers from a standard wall outlet, drawing 1100W. 3233 The Crimson uses the 14-slot MP backplane from the PowerSeries, which has three buses—PowerPath, VME, and graphics—each with specific board roles and limitations. 34 It runs sash.IP17 and fx.IP17 firmware, with half the PROM on the CPU board and half on the IO board, and requires the io3 board for a functional VME bus. 3536 The Crimson shares a PSU with the Fuji PEX479, and a PowerOne PSU can serve as a replacement for the Cherokee PSU. 3738 The Crimson is unreliable, with the Reality Engine cited as the unreliable component and reports that it has only worked a few times despite extensive spare parts. 3940 The Crimson is a vintage SGI system from the PowerSeries line, using an R4k CPU and the older PROM, and it is essentially the last iteration of the PowerSeries, which it can be upgraded from. 414243 A PowerSeries PSU (SPM5ADAELBS180) can be used in a Crimson after swapping the wiring harness, though a different harness is required for startup. 44 The Crimson's power inlet receptacle allows voltage selection at the PSU via bridged terminals '1' and '2', with 230VAC indicated by opening the terminals. 45 The Crimson is associated with Jurassic Park and was a popular SGI system. 46 The Crimson's diagnostic 0x0E failure occurs during the Data Cache - Address Test after failing the low memory test, causing LEDs 5, 6, and 7 to light up. 47 The Crimson has a multiprocessor probe that polls for a 0xBAAD flag in RAM for tens of seconds, a delay that serves no purpose in finding additional processor boards. 48 The IRIX 6.2 media have a bug that prevents installation on a blank disk on a Crimson, which can be resolved with a specific patch set. 4950 The Graphics Manager Board has buried tantalum capacitors that need replacement in Crimson. 51
