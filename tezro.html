Tezro

Claims: 314 Background: 10 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI Tezro, SGI Fuel, SGI Octane.

With Fuel

The Fuel and Tezro are SGI workstations in tower form factors that were the last MIPS SGI machines, existing due to Itanium delays and Fuel underperformance. 1234 They use XIO for their V12's and DM3 cards, with edge connectors, and share underlying technology and components with the Octane. 567 Both have standard DVI output, use a ST micro or DS1742W for system time with a replaceable battery/crystal module, and support USB, though dual-seat functionality via USB is not possible. 89101112 The Tezro has GigE, RSE, and can upgrade to a single processor, while the Fuel does not have GigE and cannot be upgraded. 131415 They use 68-pin optical drives and can use official firewire cards, with 1000Base-SX cards also working. 161718 They use proprietary RAM similar to the O350, have L1 controllers like the Origin 3000, and use the same nodeboards as other Chimera systems. 1920 USB HID works on the Fuel, Tezro, Onyx4, and other systems, as IRIX converts the USB HID to PS/2 internally. 21 The Fuel's maximum speed was 900 MHz, and 1 GHz Tezro models were similarly unadvertised. 22 A Dallas DS1742W chip can be sourced from SmallRun.net or from a forum member in Europe for SGI Fuel, Tezro, and O350 systems. 23

With Octane

The Tezro is a stretched-out Octane that can fit two more processors, is taller, wider and deeper than an Octane, and weighs around 65 lbs, making it heavier than Octanes and Onyx2s. 24252627 The Tezro and Octane can support up to 8 GB of RAM, and the Tezro was built around XIO having a large bandwidth for multi-processor support in tasks like video editing. 282930 The Octane with a V12 CPU is better than a Tezro, and the V12 CPU in an Octane2 is the same as in the Tezro with the same performance. 313233 The Octane's system bus throughput was technically superior to the Tezro, reaching up to 700 MB/s with multiple SCSI cards. 34 The Octane can handle very high resolution playback, but H.264 at high resolution can cause issues, even on a Tezro. 35 The Tezro and Octane use RAM from FromJapan, and RSE is biased towards 6.5.30 with multiple disks recommended for Tezro or Octane users for Flame. 3637

With Origin 350

The Tezro is essentially an Origin 350 in a case with graphics, and is similar to other Chimera systems. 38 The Origin 350 and Tezro use the same CPU board; the Tezro has more PCI slots and onboard video. 39 Tezro, O350, Altix 350, and Prism use the same sleds. 40 The Tezro and O350 have built-in gigabit Ethernet, which is useful for the O350. 41 Transferring a HDD from a Tezro to an O350 should allow the O350 to boot, assuming the O350 has a VPro. 42 Cloning a drive with VPro from an O350 to a Tezro should work. 43 An Origin 350 can be modified into a Tezro with a V10/V12 card. 44 Tezro bricks require a special serial number to work with a router, otherwise they are equivalent to O350s without graphics. 45

With Onyx350

The rackmount Tezro is an Onyx 350 that was factory-crippled to be less expandable, with a processor limit of four and a memory limit of 8GB, and can only be installed in a system with two bricks or less. 464748 The rack Tezro locks out CPU numalink and only provides more PCI slots if numalinked, whereas the desktop Tezro has more PCI slots than the rack Tezro. 4950 The rack Tezro can be converted to a rack Onyx350 and linked with seven more bricks. 51 An Onyx350 is a Tezro and a V12 in a 2U enclosure, and you can swap the CPU board between a Tezro and an Onyx350. 5253 The Tezro has an IO card slot like the Onyx350, which supports 3.3v or 5v. 54

General

The Tezro is a deskside SGI system that is not an Onyx, and the Prism is essentially a Tezro chassis with different skins. 5556 It is a desktop with more integral PCI slots and onboard video always included. 57 The Tezro is significantly faster than other SGI systems due to its quad CPUs and optimized R16K MIPS CPUs, performing better than a machine with four CPUs when running graphically intensive applications. 5859 It is faster than a C8000 workstation, and only the Tezro and O3000 could do 1 GB/second. 6061 The Tezro can handle 720p xvid without issues and boots to IRIX, operating as an independent system. 6263 The Tezro has a whine that can be annoying. 64 The Tezro is a true 64-bit system that is more capable than the Prism, since Itanium was left out at release. 6566 It supports USB peripherals such as keyboards, mice, and audio devices via a PCI card, but not USB mass storage. 6768 The Tezro runs the second revision of VPro graphics, which runs at double the clock speed of the first-generation VPro and is on a 1GHz node board. 69 It has 3 DVI ports, with the top left used for the console. 70 The Tezro is a large system, weighing about 30-35kg. 71 It can have NVRAM issues that need programming to fix, and the hostid cannot be changed. 7273 The Tezro is highly prized as the last and most powerful SGI MIPS workstation, still used in production. 74 It was used by Technicolor for film transfer using Phantom. 7576 It can support more than 4 CPUs and 8 GB of RAM. 77 It has eight fans, including a wall of fans on the PCI side and three more externally mounted for the node, one for the hard disk bays. 78 The Tezro desktop has known heat issues, especially with VRMs. 79 It uses a Dallas DS1742W timekeeper chip on the PCI interface board running L1. 8081 It requires loading the kernel over network using TFTP because it lacks a spinning SCSI disk and needs a loaded SATA driver. 82 It can use USB keyboards and mice, but USB mass storage is not supported. 83 The Tezro was an expensive, underpowered machine compared to PCs of its era, particularly in graphics, and it lacked DDR2 RAM. 84 It did not sell well, and its price increased significantly around 2019 as it became extremely rare. 8586 The Tezro was primarily used for Flame in post production houses, mainly for video editing, and was necessary for Discreet's software until a reliable Linux port was available. 8788 SGI only officially offered the Tezro with V12 graphics, which does not run with FireGL cards. 89 The Tezro is reliable but not always easy to find, and a deskside model is not necessary. 90 The Tezro is the top model for user workstations and is more expandable than the O2, though parts are harder to find if something goes wrong. 91 It is heavy and loud, with small casters that are not useful due to its weight, and it defies categorization, being similar to the prism in design. 92 The Tezro is fast but not reliable or quiet, and it is not recommended for daily use. 92 The Tezro and Onyx models come in various shades of purple. 93 The VPro and FireGL are common in the Onyx line, which is essentially a rackmount Tezro. 94 The rackmount Tezro is a better value than the desktop version, being more reliable, running cooler, having more features, and being better built. 95 The Tezro can sell for 15k and is desired by some. 9697 Tezro and Vizrt are particularly rare. 98
