Fuel

Claims: 285 Background: 11 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI Fuel, Power supply unit (computer), SGI O2, SGI Octane.

With Tezro

The Fuel and Tezro are the last MIPS SGI workstations, sharing the ip35 platform and using XIO for their V12 and DM3 cards via edge connectors. 1234 Both systems use the same nodeboards as other Chimera systems and have L1 controllers like the Origin 3000. 5 They use proprietary RAM, standard DVI output, and 68-pin optical drives, and both sold official firewire cards. 6789 The Tezro has GigE and RSE and can run compilers, while the Fuel is nearly silent with an ATX PSU but lacks GigE. 1011 The Tezro can be upgraded to a single processor, but the Fuel cannot. 12 Both systems use a ST micro for system time with a replaceable battery/crystal module, and the L1 firmware allows changing the date and time before the PROM or OS boots. 1314 They can use USB, though only barely, and dual-seat functionality via USB is not possible. 1516 The Fuel's maximum speed was 900 MHz and 1 GHz Tezro models were similarly unadvertised. 17

With Octane

The Fuel and Octane are SGI machines in tower form factor, sharing underlying technology and components that were repackaged for different markets. 181920 The Fuel has 4GB RAM and a V12 processor, compared to an Octane with 256MB RAM, and it has a faster memory subsystem based on IP35 technology. 2122 The Fuel is faster than the vast majority of Octane configurations, including in its slowest configuration, and has a faster hard drive controller and more PCI slots. 2324 The Octane is more reliable than the Fuel, and the Fuel is a better system than all but the most incredibly high-end Octanes. 252627 The parts for an Octane are more expensive than a Fuel, and it is easier to get hands on a Fuel or Octane2, usually at a lower cost. 2829 The Octane is more used than the Fuel, despite the Fuel being more powerful. 30 The Fuel is incompatible with the O350, so a dual CPU Octane is a better choice. 31 The Fuel supports SATA cards while the Octane does not, and both can support gigabit Ethernet. 3233 The Fuel was cheaper to manufacture and possibly aimed at customers looking for a cheaper alternative to the Octane2. 34 The Fuel is easier to upgrade with 2 GB of RAM being a cheap option compared to the Octane. 35 Octane and Fuel benefit from 15k drives. 36

With O2

The Fuel is an upgrade for the O2, not the Octane. 37 The Fuel is sometimes perceived as the successor to the SGI O2, but it is not, as SGI never made a new low-end system after the O2. 38 The Fuel was SGI's mid-range response to customers who only wanted a uniprocessor system. 38 An O2 cannot achieve acceptable framerate for 640x480 DVD playback, but a Fuel can do so. 39 The Lamer Intro runs at 60 FPS on the Fuel and 43 FPS with a mod, and performs poorly on the O2. 40 The O2 is criticized for being slow but is better for media than the Indy and Fuel. 41

General

The SGI Fuel is a tower workstation that runs IRIX and was introduced in January 2002. 4243 It is a midrange machine with a 700 MHz R16k CPU, 4 GB RAM (which is the maximum), and a 146 GB 10k RPM drive. 444546 It is the only SGI tower that runs IRIX, and it can be cheaper than an Origin while being faster. 4347 The Fuel has a standard front-to-back airflow design, but its thermal design is less effective than the desktop due to its GPU getting hot air. 4849 The Fuel's PSU issues are easier to fix than most SGI's, but it has issues with original power supplies that can fail in rough ways. 5046 Some Fuel workstations had PSUs manufactured by Sparkle, which explains their failure issues, and even non-Sparkle PSUs had voltage spike problems. 51 The Fuel's CPU can experience voltage issues which may be related to the PSU, and replacing the CPU and using an ATX adapter resolved the issue. 52 The SGI Fuel is a personal project that serves as a replacement for a single CPU Octane, combining features of midrange to high-end configurations into a single-CPU system alongside the 1500. 535455 The most basic Fuel had a 500 MHz R14000 with a V10 CPU, and the case design resembles a tooth, continuing a design lineage from the Indigo1 to the Fuel. 5657 The Fuel supports PCI-X, U160, later ip35-infrastructure cards (SAS), USB, and DVI output, and has PS/2 ports, many PCI slots, and an internal serial port usable for L1 access. 5859606162 The Fuel has a 430W PSU, which can be replaced with an ATX PSU using an adapter, and it is the only SGI where ATX swaps are commonly done. 636465 The Fuel is relatively quiet with slow fans, and a quiet ATX power supply can further reduce noise. 6667 The Fuel could only support up to 8GB of RAM and can run SSDs, which override most other settings. 6869 The Fuel can boot with 10MB/sec sound card performance and can netboot, as one user netbooted because they lacked a SCSI drive and wanted less noise. 7071 The Fuel can run at 1920x1080 with 60hz, though this requires reducing color depth to 16 bit. 72 The SGI Fuel is a workstation based on a single-CPU Origin 300 in an ATX case with graphics, and it shares the IP35 platform with the Origin 300. 737475 It uses PC1600 DDR-200MHz Registered ECC CL2 184-Pin DIMM 2.5V RAM, which maxes out at 4GB and is shared with the O350, O300, and Tezro. 7677 The Fuel has a fan controller, two LCDs (one resembling the Apple G4's display), DVI output, faster built-in SCSI and USB support, and is the only model with USB built in. 78798081 It lacks onboard audio hardware, unlike generic PCs of the time and unlike the V12, which can use RAD audio. 8283 The Fuel is notorious for failing power supplies, environment monitoring issues, boot problems, OS corruption, and a temper SCSI-68 Pin cable that cannot be replaced. 84858687 Its L1 controller can only be accessed via a direct connection or the x86-based L2 remote management system, and its serial console is routed to a serial port on the back panel rather than the L1. 8889 The SGI Fuel is a single-CPU machine with a 4 GB memory limit, and it is considered mid-range, faster than all models except the latest dual-CPU Octanes at the high end. 90919293 The Fuel has a blue case, a 'Turbo Air' air shroud, and early firmware, with a 500 MHz V10 CPU. 94 The Fuel requires a PSU with lower voltage rails, which are less common on modern ATX PSUs, and replacing the original power supply with one matching the original spec is recommended. 9596 The Fuel has a common failure point involving the environmental monitor, which can affect PCI and USB functionality. 97 The Fuel cannot boot from SATA, and the LSI SATA controller works with older 3Gbps SSDs but is picky about compatibility. 9899100 The Fuel lacks audio hardware, and software support for FireWire was incomplete. 101102
