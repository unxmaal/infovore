XZ

Claims: 40 Background: 1 claim already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI Indy.

With R5000

  • XZ includes a geometry engine and on R4k it's faster than the CPU FP, but on R5k it's slower. (+6 similar) (user-d903, exchange 101120, 2021-12-07 to 2026-02-15; 6 speaker(s), 5 conversation(s))
  • The XZ requires an R4k or R5k for optimal performance. (user-24f0, exchange 224934, 2025-04-21; 1 speaker(s), 1 conversation(s))
  • The r5000 with XL 24bit mode can perform geometry transforms faster than the XZ but cannot use the CPU for other tasks simultaneously. (user-6afa, exchange 224612, 2025-02-17; 1 speaker(s), 1 conversation(s))
  • an R5000 with XL performs better 'in many cases' than XZ, which implies that it depends on the particular situation (user-c383, exchange 204996, 2021-04-23; 1 speaker(s), 1 conversation(s))

General

The XZ includes a geometry engine that handles transformations in hardware, and it has four geometry engines but less Z buffer. 123 On R4k processors the XZ is faster than the CPU for geometry tasks, but on R5k processors it is slower and the R5k can software render 3D faster. 145 Installing an XZ on an R5k system can reduce 3D performance compared to XL, and it makes the system less efficient for geometry tasks but still faster for interactive logic and polygon pushing. 67 The XL24 is a single-height board that performs better than the double-height XZ for R5000 CPUs in many cases, and the R5000 Indy is best with a single-height XL24 board. 8910 Using an XZ involves a trade-off where you get hardware 3D but lose 2D performance and expansion, and depending on workload it can be slower than letting the R5k do all of the work. 1112 The XZ requires an R4k or R5k for optimal performance and a PROM version newer than July 1994 to work. 513
