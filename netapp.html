NetApp

Claims: 13

NetApp creates a filesystem (WAFL) on top of RAID on whatever disks you put in an aggregate, and you can make a volume that serves as a filesystem for NAS or create a LUN in a volume presented via iSCSI or FC. 1 NetApp does not use dedicated hardware for RAID; it runs as a dedicated and isolated process in the OS, and its RAID-DP implementation (RAID 6, dual parity) is solid. 2 By 2007, NetApp had FC backplanes and allowed mixing drive speeds and sizes within an enclosure, whereas early EMC used spindle sync. 3 Hitachi and NetApp branded SSDs sometimes lack the ability to set sector size, requiring careful low-level formatting, and some older NetApp drives show 520B/sector. 4 Reformatting NetApp and EMC HDDs to 512-byte sectors usually works, but some SAS disks may fail if not zeroed first. 5 FusionIO cards have 75PBW endurance on SLC flash and were OEMed by Sandisk and NetApp. 6 The Indy can connect to a NetApp via 10Gb Ethernet with NFS, but it has buffering and TCP flow control issues. 7 NetApp can run with one socket and half the RAM. 8
