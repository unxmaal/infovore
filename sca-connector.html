SCA connector

Claims: 19 Part of SCA

The SCA connector carries data and power and is used with various vintage computers. 1 The SCA connector on the backplane does not have the same pinout as SCSI drives, even though the system assigns SCSI IDs to each drive bay. 2 The SCA connector on Octane, O2, and newer systems is not pin-compatible with SCA80, Ultra-SCSI, or Fast-Wide SCSI hard disk pinouts, risking power shorts if misused. 3 The SCA connector on the drive caddy sticks out 2mm too far, preventing the front latch from engaging the locking pins. 4 The sled is 2.5mm thick and the SCA connector is 1.6mm, and the SCA connector on Indigo2 disks is incompatible due to height despite having an identical pinout. 56 The backplane board's receiving end uses a standard SCA connector, but the caddy side is not physically compatible. 7 The I2 has an SCA connector on the backplane but most sleds don't actually link the drive directly to it. 8 SCA connectors are difficult to obtain, costing around $30-40 and requiring significant effort. 9
