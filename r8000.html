R8000

Claims: 88 Background: 6 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see R8000, SGI Indy, SGI Onyx.

With R10000

The R8000 combines the R3000's core with the R10000's FPU, making it suitable for floating-point intensive tasks like 3D and scientific computing, and was introduced in 1994 for SGI workstations and servers before being largely replaced by the R10000 in 1996. 12 The R8000 was a multi-chip CPU with a higher heat flux density, while the R10000 integrated everything in one chip with out-of-order execution and pipelining. 34 The R8000 and R10000 are part of a later architecture version, and both required IRIX 6.x, with the R8K requiring 6.0 or later. 56 The R8000 excels in highly-optimized FP matrix computations and specific 90s scientific workflows, while the R10000 outperforms it in general computing and is generally superior in most applications. 78 The R8000 outperforms the earliest R10000s in floating-point operations only at 200 MHz and lower clock rates, but the R10000 is a far better all-round CPU. 910 Many R8000 systems had hardware bugs and reliability issues, leading SGI to replace many with R10000s. 1112

General

The R8000 is a multichip processor codenamed TFP for Tremendous Floating Point, designed for tremendous floating-point performance rather than graphics. 1314 It implements the MIPS4 instruction set, which includes reciprocal and reciprocal square root instructions. 15 The R8000 has a 64-bit address bus, is superscalar with 4-way issue, and has multiple units for integer and FPU operations, whereas the R4400 has a 32-bit address bus, is scalar, and has 8 pipeline stages. 16 The R8000 has limited speed due to deviating from usual design to prove something, then going back to business as usual. 17 The R8000 was not popular, used primarily for floating point operations. 18 The R8000 has floating point data that bypass the L1 cache and can perform two fused multiply-add operations per cycle for about 240 MFLOPS at 75 MHz. 19 The R8000 had 2.6 million transistors measuring 299.98 mm², and the R16000A has more than double the transistor count while being fundamentally limited to around the same floating-point operations per clock. 2021 The R8000 can achieve 3.083 FP64 instructions per clock cycle at 75 MHz, or 6.166 GFLOPS/mm² at 2 GHz, and could dissipate less than 13W. 2223 The SGI 6.0 operating system supported the 64-bit Power Challenge R8000, which was the first 64-bit kernel, and version 6.1 is compatible with R8K machines. 2425 The R8000 has a dual slot Impact card, and Teton was the codename for the R8000 Indigo2. 2627 The R8000 is not reliable for installation, and the Teal case should have an R8K FCC sticker. 2829
