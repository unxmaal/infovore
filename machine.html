For serving, a single SGI machine is a single failure domain requiring maintenance windows for upgrades, has a poor price/performance ratio, and doesn't scale well; the future lies in cheap, loosely coupled systems. 1 MAME emulation is mostly single-threaded, so a multi-core CPU with high single-core performance is ideal for emulating an SGI machine. 2 An IBM machine with an orange gas-plasma screen was used as a terminal for the O2. 3 The boot process on an SGI machine will attempt to boot, detect no install media, prompt for key press, and present a menu with command line, install, or diagnostics options. 4 On an SGI machine, you can eject a drive by clicking its icon and selecting 'Eject' from the Special menu or dragging it to the trash can. 5 To connect an SGI machine via USB, it emulates an L2 controller; it requires Red Hat 6.2, not RHEL 6.2, and might work on Altix if the L1 controllers are similar. 6 The AC converter is a small square brick with a 2-prong plug, not related to laptops or PCs. 7 Multithreaded compiles do not work on this machine. 8 The machine has PCI and ISA slots, and a SCSI connector that may not be actual SCSI. 9 The machine is heavy and difficult to move, comparable in weight to a Tezro, and its form factor and lack of plastic to break are great advantages. 1011 The machine lacks original components except for the 'please don't talk about it' stickers from the R5000 prototypes. 12 Batteries for the machine can be replaced, but the power supply may need to be recapped, and the power supply unit is ATX. 1311
