PCI

Claims: 93 Background: 14 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see PCI-X, Accelerated Graphics Port, NVM Express, PCI Express, SGI Octane.

SGI initially resisted PCI, but later supported it on machines after the Indigo2. 1 The Sun Ultra 5 and 10 are single-processor systems with PCI, while the Ultra 2 is dual-processor with SBus. 2 The O350 has 4 PCI slots and can mount graphics. 3 The Ultra 10 towers have a UPA slot and some PCI. 4 The Octane can optionally support PCI cards. 5 The G5 tower supports PCI, PCI-X, and PCIe. 6 PCI-X doubles the maximum clock speed of conventional PCI from 66 MHz to 133 MHz, increasing the theoretical maximum data exchange between the processor and peripherals to 1.06 GB/s. 7 All PCI-X is 64 bit, but not all PCI is 64 bit, though both can be 64 bit. 8 PCI is a shared bus in which all slots share bandwidth, making it likely slower than PCI-X, which is suitable for video capture and DAW tasks. 91011 PCI is limited to ~100MB/s, while PCIe uses a star network and requires a PHY unlike PCI. 121314 PCI and AGP use GART, while PCIe uses its own GART or full VM system, and PCI signaling is shared except for bus req/grant and cfgreg. 1516 A PCI to PCIe adapter can be used for NVMe support. 17 PCI has more bandwidth than FDDI. 18 The PCI bracket spacings follow the Intel ATX spec or the original IBM AT form factor, though finding authoritative sources is challenging. 19 PISA is a combo PCI+ISA slot that also works directly for ISA cards, using a connector similar to EISA for ISA compatibility. 20 VLB and Fast Ethernet coexisted with PCI and EISA, with VLB being the low-end option. 21 VLB cards, especially low-end ones, were often more expensive and sought after than their PCI or EISA counterparts. 22 486 motherboards with PCI were rare, making VLB graphics cards more interesting for 486 systems. 23 The PCI vendor ID for Intel is 0x8086. 24 The interface board has XIO and PCI slots. 25
