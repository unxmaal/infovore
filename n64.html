N64

Claims: 134 Background: 12 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see Nintendo 64, Silicon Graphics, IRIS GL, RDRAM, Video games and Linux.

With O2

The N64 and O2 share architectural similarities: both use an embedded R4000-class MIPS CPU and unified memory, and the O2's architecture is very similar to the N64, making it potentially usable for N64 development. 12 The O2 uses a hardware design similar to the N64, putting another MIPS CPU into the memory map with some 6K of firmware, and the N64 chip is a subset of the O2 one. 34 The O2 uses the CRIME engine for memory, video, and compression, handling vector calculations with a separate CPU, while the N64 uses the RSP for vector calculations. 5 The O2's unified memory map is one that N64 emulators had trouble emulating. 6 The O2 performs shading in a second pass and may not support texture blending effects from the N64 due to OGL compliance issues. 7 The N64 video output may not work on SGI O2 due to sync signal differences from the PAL specification, similar to Atari 800 and IBM PC (CGA). 8 Someone got an N64 working on an Indy and an O2. 9

General

The N64 uses a MIPS CPU developed by MIPS and SGI, and cannot run x86/x64 software directly. 101112 It has a maximum RAM of 8 MB and a maximum ROM size of 240 MB, with 4 KB of texture memory often split into 2 KB for texture and 2 KB for palette. 1314 The N64 uses an RSP similar in concept to the CRM graphics of the O2, which processes RSP-encoded data for display lists flushed to the RSP every frame as a GFX task. 1516 Most games of the era were written in C, with assembly still common and C++ rarely used due to overhead and limited memory, and MIPS assembly is used in N64 development. 1718 Several developers have created their own SDK/OS for the N64 using C, and coding an OS might be easier than porting one. 1920 Non-game software on the N64 is specialized; it has good specs for Win 3.1 but not Win95, and running Linux is impractical due to limited RAM and lack of necessary peripherals. 2122 IRIX would be very difficult to run on the N64 without source code and a significant amount of memory, and trying to get IRIX working is not practical without source code. 2324 There is a custom command-based OS written in Assembly that might work on the N64, requiring less than a megabyte of RAM to boot. 25 The N64 development SDK was designed for SGI workstations, including the Indy, Onyx, and O2, due to their similar architectures, and Nintendo PARTNER-N64 was a development kit for SGI workstations that connected to a special N64 via SCSI for game development. 2627 IRIX does not run on the N64, though IRISGL is an OpenGL implementation for SGI machines that can run N64 software with modifications. 2829 The N64's RCP runs a MIPS R4k series core and uses SGI UMA, and the console was a scaled-down InfiniteReality. 3031 A stock N64 has 4 MB of texture RAM, which is its total RAM capacity, and it had a 4k texture cache that likely did not support multitexturing. 3233 The N64's CPU has no DMA because the RCP has to do it, and the RDP has complex calls that require learning. 3435 The N64 doesn't have sprites in the conventional sense; even HUD elements are just a pair of polygons with a texture on them. 36 The N64 outputs only S-Video and composite, so a passive adapter is needed for component input, and its video output is not ideal but can be managed with the right hardware. 3738 N64s are prone to freezing if the cartridge connection loses contact for even a second, but they can survive being mishandled and remain functional. 3940 The N64 is a low-end MIPS system with Onyx graphics features, slower than many SGI systems, and had hardware textures that some SGI systems lacked. 41 It had some unique graphics features but was much slower than many SGI systems. 42 SGI used RDRAM and licensed N64 technology, and the N64's graphics architecture, or one of its architects, influenced SGI's design philosophy. 4344 The N64 shipped significantly more units than SGI's computer systems over their history. 45 The N64 binaries are statically linked only, and MIPSpro does not support N64 and produces invalid code for older CPU steppings. 4647 The N64 can experience a 'power on, no video or audio' issue that persists despite cleaning and replacing AV cables, with the board looking clean and without obvious solder point or capacitor corrosion. 48 Its RDRAM can be finicky, especially with texture RAM, and troubleshooting may involve contact cleaning or repairing corroded traces near the CPU or RCP. 4950 Some N64 models converted from quads to triangles for performance can misalign textures, leading to visual issues like stretched or broken textures, especially on full quad textures such as pillars in Pokémon Stadium. 51
