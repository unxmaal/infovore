RM7000

Claims: 29 Background: 1 claim already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI O2.

The RM7000 is a MIPS processor that was used in Cisco routers (such as the NPE-300, NPE-400, and NPE-400) with clock speeds of 262 MHz and 350 MHz, and in SGI systems. 123 The RM7000 is comparable to the R10K processor but not as fast, and it reached clock speeds up to 800 MHz. 45 The RM5200 is a die shrink of the RM7000 with higher clock speeds and more cache, and the RM7000 kept data registers the same as the 5200. 67 The RM7000 and RM5271 are 99.7% pin compatible, differing only in one cache-related pin, and the RM7900 is pin-compatible with the RM7000 but has a different core architecture and lacks L3 cache pins. 89 The RM7000 has 3.6 million transistors and runs cooler, fitting better than its predecessors. 1011 The RM7000 was used as a replacement CPU for the SGI O2, but the O2's ip32PROM requires specific CPU chip registers and data values to boot correctly, with different requirements for RM7900 compared to RM7000 chips. 1213 An IRIX 6.5.30 installation on an Octane may not boot on an O2 with an RM7000, and running 6.5 on an RM7000 is not slow. 1415 Fake RM7000, RM7000A, and RM7000B CPUs can be used but may not work properly without system PROM work, and the markings on a supposed RM7000 can look off. 1617
