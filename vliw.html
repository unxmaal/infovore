VLIW

Claims: 6

VLIW is an explicit superscalar architecture that avoids complex scheduling logic by explicitly stating which operations can be done in parallel, rather than relying on microcode to schedule instructions. 1 It is good for running start-to-finish operations in parallel, but can become inefficient with conditional execution or branching due to the need for tight control over different units. 2 VLIW can act as a pipeline through software instruction streams, but does not inherently imply a deep hardware pipeline. 3 Itanium was not x86 and building VLIW compilers was difficult, leading to performance and efficiency issues. 4 AMD's shader cores moved away from VLIW due to complexity issues. 5 A VLIW DSP used in a job has no ALU forwarding, jumps, and only conditional execution. 6
