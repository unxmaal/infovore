NT

Claims: 75 Background: 2 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see Windows NT.

NT was based on Mica, which later became Alpha, and was designed and implemented by DEC engineers that Microsoft poached; it was not descended from VMS. 1234 NT was designed to be portable and ran on IA64 processors before being abandoned in 1999 for RISC ports. 5 NT was its own original kernel with no OS/2 parts in the kernel at all, though it had an OS/2 userspace personality. 6 NT was highly modular, microkernel-based, portable, and more elegant than Unix in its early versions, but its design deteriorated over time. 7 NT is preemptive with a HAL, which makes the system more stable, and it cannot swap kernel stacks the way Linux can. 89 NT was technically inferior to Unix in the 90s but took over the datacenter. 10 NT had compatibility issues with Win16 applications. 11 NT was formerly expanded to "New Technology" but no longer carries any specific meaning. 12 NT was developed by Microsoft, who hired DEC's best OS engineers for the project. 13 NT supported multiple architectures and operating systems, including Tru64, VMS, Unix, and Linux, and could run on machines such as the PC164 mainboard, SGI machines, and the SNI RM200. 14151617 NT required specific hardware abstraction layers (HALs) for different machines, and different ECU versions existed for NT and Unix/VMS. 1819 NT supported a POSIX subsystem and an x86 emulator on Alpha. 2021 NT used a partition scheme with a small FAT partition for the bootloader and a larger NTFS partition for the system. 22 NT was supported by various software, including Exceed, an X server program that provided an X11 display, and was used by applications such as Softimage and Unigraphics. 232425
