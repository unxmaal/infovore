486

Claims: 51 Background: 1 claim already covered by Wikipedia, see Graphics card.

The 486 was a point at which MIPS, POWER and SPARC were no longer laughably far behind for integer performance, though by the middle of the decade they still outperformed P5-based designs and most P6-based ones. 1 The 486's FPU performance was poor, which is why Sun or SGI machines were preferred for such work. 2 The 486 was concurrent with the early Lunchbox models. 3 The DX4 Overdrive was a great upgrade for early 486 boards. 4 The 486 can run classic games, and upgrading the video card can improve the frame rate. 56 Good candidates for a 486 include OS/2, DOS, some older versions of Linux/BSD, QNX, and Windows 9x. 7 More than 32 MB of RAM on a 486 can negatively affect performance due to limited cache. 8 The 486 with VLB can run stably for years without issues. 9 ECP and EPP were supported on 386 and 486 definitely. 10 A 486 with defaulting memory chips can show vertical stripes and become hot. 11 KM416C256BJ-6 memory chips can be used to replace onboard graphics memory in a 486. 12
