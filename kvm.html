KVM

Claims: 51 Background: 6 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see KVM switch, QEMU.

KVM and bhyve are well-established hypervisors that have received significant development effort over the last decade, making them suitable recommendations for modern use, and they are used for VMs. 12 Proxmox uses KVM and LXC containers. 3 QEMU without KVM is only good for different architectures or really old OSes. 4 OpenVZ limits optimization potential due to lack of kernel control, and VMs are not always well-integrated with the host system, with KVM being an exception. 56 KVMs struggle with connecting diverse hardware. 7 KVM can run multiple GPUs, but they must be from the same vendor, and it can mix Intel and AMD by downgrading the CPU to a generic x64, while EVC masks CPUID features to provide a common baseline. 89 Linux was first to hide CPUID in KVM, possibly before KVM was mainlined. 10 Dilos supports KVM and SPARC. 11 IRIX switches keyboards into scancode 3, which many KVMs do not support from the start. 12 The only reliable KVMs are old datacenter units such as Avocent, which were designed to work with IRIX. 13 The keyboard issues persist with and without the KVM, and the symptoms are consistent across different firmware versions. 14 Swapping a SIP module with different firmware made the KVM work properly. 15 Adding error parity error checking and checking for a valid start bit fixed keyboard issues with the KVM. 16
