Onyx

Claims: 187 Background: 13 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see SGI Onyx, Silicon Graphics, Graphics processing unit, IRIX, SGI Onyx2.

With Crimson

The Onyx is the successor to the Crimson, differing from it in CPU count and overall capability, with the Crimson having only one R4000 CPU while the Onyx can have multiple CPUs and being more advanced in every way. 12 The Sun Onyx is heavier than the Sun Crimson due to its drive arrangement and more VME cards, and its backplane functions as a backplane, unlike the Crimson, which requires additional wiring and connectors between VME cards and the front bulkhead. 34 The Onyx is faster and more integrated than the Crimson, with fewer wires and no fan noise, and it never caught fire. 5 The Onyx can be upgraded with InfiniteReality or a Crimson to RealityEngine. 6 Onyx is a reliability nightmare, while Crimson is pretty solid except for RealityEngine, and Onyx2 is much more reliable. 7 The PowerChallenge can fail when rebuilding a Crimson, and the process of restoring an Onyx can also be disrupted by hardware failures. 8

General

The Onyx is a Tezro and one of the deskside workstations, alongside other 4D desksides. 910 Rare used an Onyx for their N64 development system, and it was used for heavy lifting during early N64 development, including rendering. 1112 Onyx systems ran the N64 simulator until hardware was manufactured, and GoldenEye 64 was initially made on an Onyx. 1314 An Onyx, Octane, or newer SGI system can emulate a N64 adequately for playing games. 15 The Onyx was $250k, and a deskside Onyx is desirable. 1617 The Onyx is not recommended due to its proprietary mouse+keyboard, questionable reliability, and high weight. 18 The Onyx uses SGI-protocol keyboards, which are DB15 and hard to find, making setup potentially difficult. 19 The Onyx requires 1500W and a 21" monitor needs 175W, and it can run off a single 15A household outlet with no issues, but requires a dedicated circuit for higher power usage. 2021 The Onyx is a complete SGI system that uses R8000 or R10K processors, with dual R8000 models featuring Reality2 (RE2) graphics and dual R10K 195 MHz CPUs. 2223 The IP21 CPU board in the Onyx uses one or two R8000 microprocessor sets with a customized cache controller, separate floating point unit, and two tag RAM and two SRAM cache units. 24 The Onyx typically uses R8K processors, indicated by IP21. 25 The Reality Station was a specific SKU of the Onyx with a single CPU but full RE2 graphics subsystem, and its firmware could be modified to support four processors. 26 The Onyx runs IRIX 5.3 with a quad R4K/RE2 CPU. 27 The Onyx supports the IO3B, which is very capable for a deskside system. 28 Some Onyx systems shipped with one HVD bus and one LV SE (SCSI-2) controller, allowing for at least two controllers, and Onyx sleds are capable of multichannel SCSI in addition to running as Differential or SE. 2930 The Onyx had a HIPPI card and was used for video media, but required a network partner to connect. 3132 The Onyx is a large SGI system that was part of the usual N64 devkit setup, used by developers to preview Nintendo 64 games with a RealityEngine. 3334 It uses a 200 MHz R4400 CPU and has less CPU and memory slots compared to the Challenge L. 3536 The Onyx is heavier than the O2K and requires more than two people to lift, but can be moved by rolling it with a handle on the back. 3738 It has built-in network capability and can be reconfigured from SE to HVD SCSI by replacing a red mezzanine card with a green one. 3940 The Onyx experienced frequent board failures, even after part replacements, and a dropped unit was left as a replacement. 41 The Onyx is a rackmount machine that is essentially a Tezro, with models available in various shades of purple and featuring purple wave panels. 424344 It has an OLS rather than a PSU, and its VPro and FireGL graphics cards are common in the line. 4544 The Onyx was expected to have higher performance and was more reliable than the 4D machines. 4647 It has audio capabilities with an add-on board, and Indigo or Onyx keyboards and mice can be used with it. 4849 The original Onyx is more of an IRIS machine architecture-wise, and it can use 68pin disks with a SCSI adapter reset. 5051 4D disks on the Onyx do not accept LVD disks, and it uses proprietary keyboards. 5253 Offloading 3D and imaging operations to the GPU can improve usability on the Onyx by accelerating the imaging and rendering pipelines on UMA systems. 54
