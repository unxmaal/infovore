x86

Claims: 106 Background: 3 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see Itanium, Oracle Solaris, X86-64.

x86 is the most universally used architecture. 1 Itanium was designed differently from x86 and was not hardware-compatible, which contributed to its decline. 2 Intel's x86 engineers could not replicate specific performance cases of Itanium, leading to an initial overestimation of its superiority and a later realization of its limitations in general use. 3 NT was originally intended for RISC workstations running MICA but ended up running on MIPS and x86. 4 Solaris 2.4 is the earliest version of Solaris that will run on x86. 5 x86 achieved massive consumer adoption, with its success attributed to price/performance and compatibility, while other RISC architectures like Alpha and Itanium were slower and more expensive. 67 Most Linux/BSD code targets x86, with graphics cards being a notable exception. 8 x86 virtualization is wonky, and x86 desktop PCs lack out-of-band management. 910 x86 has conditional jump instructions like jne, and its ISA is considered less elegant than the 68000's. 1112 x86 is already being displaced in various sectors, and it might split into two different Application Binary Interfaces if it survives. 1314 x86 became dominant due to cost and IT department preferences post-dotcom bubble. 15 The project involves porting IRIX to x86, currently chain loading another EFI application, with plans to use the firmware console for kernel loading. 16
