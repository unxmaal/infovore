R4400

Claims: 98 Background: 5 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see R4000, SGI Indigo2, SGI Indy, Silicon Graphics.

With Indigo2

The Indigo2 was introduced in 1994 with future-like specs, including a 200 MHz R4400 CPU, 256 MB RAM, and a 32 GB disk. 1 Indigo2 models have CPUs ranging from 100 MHz to 250 MHz R4000 or R4400. 2 An r4400 non-impact Indigo2 can run IRIX 5.3, but an r10k/impact Indigo2 needs IRIX 6.2 or higher. 3 IRIX 4.0.5 runs on Indigo2 with R4400 at 200 MHz and 1 MB cache but not on 250 MHz unless it's a version for 2 MB CPUs. 4 Upgrading an Indigo2 with a 250 MHz R4400 CPU may require upgrading the PROM. 5 The Indigo2 R4400 250MHz XZ was used for rendering in the first few seasons of ReBoot. 6

General

The R4400 is a MIPS CPU used in various SGI systems, including the Challenge S, Crimson, Indy, Indigo2, Onyx, and DECstation. 789101112131415 The R4400 can be ordered at clock speeds ranging from 100 MHz to 250 MHz. 91612171819 The R4400 has 1MB of L2 cache, while the R4600SC has 512K of L2 cache. 920 The R4400 is scalar with a 32-bit address bus and 8 pipeline stages, whereas the R8000 is superscalar with a 64-bit address bus and 4-way issue. 2122 The R4400 can emulate a 486 at full speed and performs similarly to a Pentium I. 232425 A 250MHz R4400 has integer performance similar to a 486 or P1 and floating-point performance closer to a P1. 25 The R4600 is not necessarily better than the R4400 and is sometimes considered a sidegrade or even slower. 2627 The R4400 can only run MIPS3, while the R8000 and higher, along with the R5000, can also execute MIPS4. 28 The base Indy model came with a 100 MHz R4000 processor, while the deluxe version had a 100 MHz R4000 with 1 MB of secondary cache. 29 The Indy was later upgraded to 150, 175, and 200 MHz R4400 processors, and eventually to higher-end options. 29 IRIX 4.0.x supports a 150MHz R4400, and IRIX 4 runs on a 2 MB R4400 processor. 3031 The R4400 is slightly older tech than the R5000 and runs a little hotter. 32 The R5k is faster than the R4400 in all cases. 33 The R8000 is comparable in integer performance to a 200 MHz R4400 and in floating-point performance to an R10000. 34 SGI offered upgrades that allowed people to swap the hardware in their cases, such as from an R4400 to an R10K or from an Express to an IMPACT, while keeping the cases and mechanical parts. 35 The R4400 was designed for multiprocessing but included additional features in the cache controller. 36 AtoR requires Irix 6.2 and at least an SGI R4400 or greater. 37 The Crimson emulator uses MAME to handle block devices and can boot to System Maintenance with the R4400 core, but requires RTC and IO3b emulation for full functionality. 38
