A500

Claims: 54

The A500 was designed for Workbench 1.2 and is one of the cheapest Amigas, sold concurrently with the STe for years without enhancements. 123 An A500 with 1MB of chip RAM can run most Amiga software up to 1990 and can be purchased for around $150. 4 Most Amiga games run well on the A500, with high-end AGA games being exceptions, and it is a good machine for someone who already owns an Amiga. 56 To run certain Amiga games, you need a FatAgnus and a 2MB Agnus chip, and some A500 models could be modified to support more than 512k of RAM, typically resulting in 1.25 MB. 78 The A500 has good build quality but needs a new RAM board, and re seating components can resolve intermittent issues. 910 The A500 can be modified to run as a bare A2000 by removing the NiCd battery and additional RAM, and can switch between NTSC and PAL with a solder jumper. 1112 You can install an accelerator card in an A500, such as the GVP A530, a 25 MHz 030 accelerator. 1314 The A500 does not have color composite video output, and network expansion is expensive for it. 1516 The A500 was significantly cheaper imported from Germany. 17 An A500 setup in good shape, including the unit, mouse, and monitor, can go for $500, and a basic setup suitable for gaming can be obtained for around £200 in the UK. 1819 The Supra drive adds to the A500's value. 20 The A500 can be accelerated with Linux, but not with Vampires, and the Terriblefire accelerator works well enough for basic use on the A500, A1200, and CD32. 2122 The 68000 CPU on the A500 can be challenging to program, as getting even a pixel on the screen requires significant effort. 23 The P200MMX can emulate an A500 with reasonable accuracy for a 604, though modern emulation is more comprehensive and slower, and early UAE couldn't emulate A500 speed on a PMMX233 with audio, worsening with complexity. 2425
