Dallas chip

Claims: 89

The Dallas chip combines a battery, a clock, and 8k SRAM, and it retains data across reboots as long as power is not completely removed. 12 It is necessary for booting past the PROM, powering on the L1, and displaying information, and its absence can prevent machines from functioning properly in a cluster or stack. 345 A failing Dallas chip can cause corrupted Ethernet addresses, garbage eaddr values, off clocks, network issues, stuck red lights, and machines refusing to run. 678910111213 The chip can be replaced with a modern variant that still exists, though it may be tricky to remove and is sometimes socketed. 141516 Replacement methods include swapping the chip, hacking it, cutting it in half to solder a new battery and clock crystal, or using refurbishment services. 17181920 Fixes may also involve setting environment variables, using raw byte write commands, or correcting clock issues with NTP. 21910 The Dallas chip stores non-volatile data and may need to be replaced, either by reseating it, swapping in a known working chip for diagnosis, or installing a new one. 22232425 Reseating the chip can resolve issues with the L1 cache, but a dead Dallas chip prevents the L1 cache from coming up. 2627 A faulty Dallas chip can cause Ethernet errors, CMOS reset and keyboard issues, and solid amber indicating possible board failure. 282930 Replacing the Dallas chip can fix issues, and doing so may require a socket to avoid damaging the board. 2531 The MAC address is hard-coded on the IO board rather than stored in the Dallas chip. 32
