CATIA

Claims: 50 Background: 2 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see CATIA.

CATIA is a parametric CAD software that is distinct from 3D modeling software used in animation and video games, and it dominates the high-end segment of the CAD market alongside NX. 12 CATIA was originally a mainframe application using graphics terminals, and its CADAM component dates back to 1965 as an internal Lockheed project. 34 CATIA was available from both Dassault Systemes and IBM, with IBM being the official distributor for some versions and supporting CATIA workstations until around 2018. 56 CATIA runs on AIX, Solaris, IRIX, and MIPS-based systems, and it can also run on RS/6000 boxes and zOS for extended support. 789101112 CATIA never had a Linux version. 13 CATIA can model PCBs but not do actual design, and it is more suited for mechanical assembly components. 1415 The 777 was modeled using CATIA, and building a physical model was needed to convince higher-ups. 16 CATIA was developed by Dassault, with IBM serving as a reseller until the early 2000s. 17 Early versions of CATIA ran on S/370 hardware using 2250/3250 vector graphics, with later versions supporting 5080 graphics. 18 On the RS/6000, CATIA required an IBM 5086 graphics terminal, which was costly and had poor performance, and CATIA systems were expensive with IBM continuing to sell mainframe-based systems for some time. 19 The location of legacy tools in CATIA is the same as it was in the UGS days after the spinoff. 20 Running CATIA through XQuartz can be problematic due to true-color colormaps and GLX issues. 21
