RISC-V

Claims: 58 Background: 4 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see RISC-V.

RISC-V is an open-source, modern ISA that has been promoted as the next big architecture for years, changing business models for CPU companies hoping to attract users away from ARM. 1234 It is optimized for low-power cases but is slower per watt and per clock cycle than ARM or AMD64, and its latest draft CPU is a 32-bit RISC-V based processor running at 128 MHz with 16 MB of RAM, 8 MB of EPROM, IDE support, 640x480 video output, and PS/2 keyboard support. 56 RISC-V has consistent execution time for every instruction, though unaligned memory accesses are exceptions handled by the OS in kernel mode in some implementations, while most implementations allow unaligned accesses. 789 It supports hardware page table walking, may have delay slots that are transparent to the programmer, and uses CSRs that are special registers accessible via special instructions rather than mapped to memory. 101112 RISC-V is doing well in the embedded market and is being adopted by many large companies, including WD, which uses it in hard disk controllers, and SiFive, which sells RISC-V SoCs for development and support. 1314151617 However, it faces challenges in general computing or HPC due to limited SoCs availability and licensing issues, with proper development boards costing hundreds or even thousands of dollars, and a RISC-V smartphone would not get off to a good start. 131819 RISC-V is expected to match x86 performance and ARM energy efficiency in about ten years, may take 5-10 years to be used heavily embedded and for mobile adoption, and could become a competitive architecture if developed further. 2021 Baikal's MIPS Baikal is still available, but future development is moving to RISC-V. 22 RISC-V is a lot like MIPS but without delay slots. 23 Modern RISC architectures have become more CISC-like, as seen in ARM, SPARC, POWER, and RISC-V. 24 The ESP32's main line is based on xtensa, but the C3 series uses RISC-V; both have been reliable for development support. 25 RISC-V and PowerPC boards are available, with SiFive providing RISC-V boards and Raptor offering PowerPC boards, though they may be sold out or overpriced. 26 The Pocket Reform offers CPU cards for various architectures, including RISC-V, NXP, and Raspberry Pi, in a compact form factor. 27 The T31_ZX has a RISC-V core and is mostly used for video; it can run little-endian and has been tested with busybox and Slackware 15. 28 The linker can output ELF binaries for RISC-V, but not bare binaries, requiring objcopy for the text segment. 29
