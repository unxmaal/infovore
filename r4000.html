R4000

Claims: 156 Background: 4 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see R4000, SGI Indigo2, SGI O2.

With R3K

The Indigo2 was launched during the IRIX 4.x era, and 4.x runs on R3k and R4k Indigo1 and early R4k Indigo2 boards. 1 The power supply was changed between R3k and R4k boards, but the backplane is the same. 2 The PSU of the R4K has a larger zener diode than the R3K, and an R3K may have an R4K supply. 34 The R3K Indigo uses a socketed CR2032 battery with an elastic strap and a drop-in replacement, while the R4K uses a soldered connection. 56 Switching from an R4k to an R3k clears the EEPROM to FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF, and putting an R3K board back results in invalid values. 78 The EEPROM location differs between R3k and R4k boards, but the 8-pin DIP containing the MAC address and environment settings is the same for both. 910 R3K works in both chassis with both graphics, but R4K does not. 11 Dead R3k and R4k system boards are available for purchase as scrap. 12

With Indigo2

The Indigo2 offers CPUs ranging from 100 MHz to 250 MHz R4000 or R4400, 75 MHz R8000, or 175/195 MHz R10000, and can be upgraded with new CPU cards, with a proposed higher-binned R4k or R5k part potentially enabling a 400 MHz R5k. 1314 The Indigo2 R4000 runs IRIX 6.5.22, and an R4k Indigo2 with extreme graphics registers as 'XZ' in HINV. 1516 The Indigo2 has different boot sounds for R4k, R8k, and R10k models, and an R4k Indigo2 produces slow network transfer sounds similar to a high-frequency modem. 1718 The Indigo2 can run games on an R10k or R4k with lower graphics quality, and Maya 4.5 is the newest version that runs on the R4k Indigo2 at usable resolution, with versions 5.0 and up requiring too much RAM. 1920 The Indigo2 with a 100 MHz R4000 is faster than the user-24f0 IRIS with a 36 MHz R3000, and the R4k Indigo2 can be revived by replacing a dead disk. 2122

With R3000

The SGI Indigo was available in two models, the R3000 (model number CMNB003, a 33MHz machine) and the R4000 (model number CMNB003B), each with a different processor and a different power supply, the R4000 supply having a higher current rating. 232425 The R4000 is faster than the R3000. 2627 On R3000 the CPU is socketed, whereas on R4000 it must be desoldered and replaced. 28 The R3000 can be upgraded to an R4000 CPU board, with the PROM routine copying the Ethernet address to the new location, but downgrading to R3000 is not easily possible. 29 The R4000-based system can run with an R3000 CPU board for a fully working system, though the R4000 board remains as a spare. 30

General

The R4000 was the first 64-bit MIPS CPU and is classified as MIPS III. 313233 It can run either endianness by changing a status register bit, and Windows NT uses more of the R4000 MMU features than IRIX. 3435 It can only execute MIPS III, not MIPS IV, which the R5000 and R8000 or higher can. 3637 The R4600 replaced the R4000 in the product lineup as a cheaper chip for midrange Indigos. 38 The Indigo2 board can have an R4000, R4400, or R4600 CPU, all with the same heatsink. 39 The Crimson is a single-processor R4000 machine, essentially an R4K Indigo1 with a large graphics subsystem, and there were R4000 and R4400 Crimson models. 404142 The R4000 Indigo1 runs IRIX 6.5.22, and NT also ran on R4000 Indigos. 4344 MAME emulates a 20 MHz R4K running SoftPC at 60% on a 4 GHz i5-4670k. 45 The R4000 CPU daughterboard can operate at 100 or 150 MHz, and some versions can be overclocked to 300 MHz. 4647 The R4000 reports its clock speed as half the oscillator clock speed due to a timing loop in the BIOS PROM code. 48 The R4000 uses a 93C56 EEPROM, which is 2Kbit and reads 256 bits or possibly 256 bytes at a time. 49 The R4000 FPU can have the flush zero bit set in the Control Status Register to clamp underflowing operations to zero and avoid exceptions. 50 MIPS-II binaries run on R4K machines. 51 Some Linux users created a 64-bit kernel port for R4000 machines. 52 The base Indy model came with a 100 MHz R4000 processor, while the deluxe version had a 100 MHz R4000 with 1 MB of secondary cache. 5354 The Indy was later upgraded to 150, 175, and 200 MHz R4400 processors. 53 The R4000 and R4400 processors have bugs in 64-bit mode that can be mitigated by compiling with -mfix-r4000 -mfix-r4400; without these options, systems may crash or hang. 55 The R4000 end-of-page errata affects R4000 processors before the 3.x revision, potentially causing segmentation faults under certain conditions. 56 The R4000 takes 72 pin FPM 36-bit wide parity SIMMs, up to 32MB in matching groups of 4, and can be upgraded easily. 57 The R4000 and R4400 processors, when under graphical load, reach a temperature that is only warm to the touch. 58 A battery replacement is needed for the R4K. 59
