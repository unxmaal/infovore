R10000

Claims: 242 Background: 14 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see R10000, SGI O2, R5000, SGI Indigo2.

With O2

The O2 is a 32-bit machine that uses the 64-bit R10K CPU. 1 The O2 can accommodate an R10K motherboard by removing two screws and turning the SCSI sled, but O2s with no drive divider cannot fit R10K or R12K motherboard assemblies. 23 PCI riser cards are needed for R10K and R12K CPUs in an O2 chassis, and swapping from R5K to R10K requires a different riser to use the PCI slot. 45 The O2 has limited airflow and may require additional cooling for the R10K CPU. 6 The O2 with R10K at 250 MHz is significantly faster than an O2 with R5K at 300 MHz due to the improved architecture. 7 The O2 with R10K at 250 MHz is a reasonable first system for beginners, with a price of about $180. 8 Given the choice, R10K is the best option for the O2 as it is cheap now and can be upgraded to R12K, but the R5K cannot. 9 An R10k installed in an O2 will boot with time-base errors but will complain, whereas an R5k will not POST. 10

With Indigo2

The Indigo2 offered different configurations of the R10000, with an Indigo2 R10K without SolidImpact being the low-end option for the R10000. 11 The Indigo2 with an R10K and Maximum IMPACT can perform well, though the GIO64 bus can be a limitation. 12 The Impact 10000 uses the R10k CPU, which is the fastest in the Indigo2 lineup. 13 The Indigo2 can scale up to 1GB of RAM with R10k CPUs and options for MGRAS graphics, though official support is 768 MB with 64 MB sticks. 1415 The Indigo2 can support a four-card video stack including an IMPACT Channel Option card. 16 SGI planned to release an Indigo2 with a 90MHz R8000 processor and IMPACT support but skipped it for the R10000. 17

With R8000

The R8000 was a multi-chip CPU introduced in 1994, combining the R3000's core with the R10000's FPU, and was used in SGI workstations and servers before being largely replaced by the R10000 in 1996. 181920 The R8000 was a transitional design between the R4K and R10K, and both the R8K and R10K are part of a later architecture version. 2122 The R8000 has better floating-point performance than the R4x00, while the R10K and successors have superior floating-point and integer performance. 23 The R8000 is comparable in integer performance to a 200 MHz R4400 and in floating-point performance to an R10000. 24 The R10K, with twice the transistors and out-of-order execution, outperforms the R8000 in general computing, but the R8000 excels in highly-optimized FP matrix computations. 25 The R8000 was suitable for floating-point intensive tasks like 3D and scientific computing, and it excels in specific 90s scientific workflows, while the R10000 is generally superior in most applications. 1826 The R8K was fragile and sensitive to static electricity, and many R8K systems had hardware bugs, leading SGI to replace many with R10Ks. 2728

With R5000

The R5K is a cut-down version of the R10K that lacks L2 cache, making 180 MHz versions particularly slow. 2930 Both processors use the MIPS4 ISA, and the R5K went from 8 to 5 pipeline stages while the R10K did not go much deeper. 3132 The R10K CPU gets much hotter than the R5K and requires a longer PCI riser and a different backplate. 3334 Upgrading from an R5K to an R10K CPU may require a rebuild, and an R5K can be installed on an R10K mainboard with the lower connectors to get two PCI slots. 3536 The R5K is tolerable on IRIX 6.5 but significantly improved with an R10K, and upgrading the CPU is more beneficial than adding RAM for an R5k. 3738

With R12000

The R12000 improves upon the R10000 microarchitecture by inserting an extra pipeline stage to improve clock frequency, increasing the number of entries in the branch history table, and modifying instruction queues. 39 The R12k architecture had improvements over the R10k, which are largely the same, with the R14 and R16 being just die shrinks. 4041 An R12000 CPU provides a significant performance boost, likely doubling the speed of an R10000 CPU from 225 MHz. 42 Upgrading from R10000 to R12000 CPUs involves replacing the CPU module, and R10k modules can be upgraded to R12k 300MHz by adjusting resistors. 4344 IRIX install may carry over on an R10k to R12k upgrade. 45

General

The R10000 (R10K) is a MIPS processor, with a 195 MHz version being one example. 46 The R10K represents a massive architectural leap over the R4K, with performance improvements primarily determined by MHz. 474849 The R10K is part of a standard architecture shared by all CPUs through the R16K, with later models indicating die shrink and faster clock speeds. 5051 The R10000 can handle up to 1 GB of memory using 128 MB sticks, with a maximum of 8 sticks per system. 52 The R10K does not include MDMX, and early units had some errata that were fixed quickly under IRIX. 5354 The R10K has similar integer performance to the UltraSPARC-II but excels in floating-point operations, and is comparable to the RM7000 but faster. 5556 The R10K is considered the final stop for MIPS development. 57 The R10000 is a Pentium-Pro-class, out-of-order superscalar CPU that can execute up to four instructions at a time, with dynamic reordering for best performance and a 5-cycle misprediction penalty. 585960 The R10000 and later machines have similar CPU boot mode code and processes, as specified in the CRIME 1.5 PDF, which details the initial boot mode code transferring to the CRIME IC to set up the SysAd. 61 CRIME 1.5 supports R10k processors, and the ICE chip is the memory controller for R10K CPUs under 200 MHz. 6263 SGI released manuals for the R10000 and R4400 processors. 64 The R10000 CPU can run for days without issues. 65 The R10000 CPU has an errata where cache modification state is not unwound on speculative misses, affecting cache coherence; disabling speculative execution can mitigate this but at a performance cost. 66 The R10000 is true 64-bit all the way through. 67 The IRIX NVMe driver requires cache invalidation after DMA operations for IO-to-MEM transfers on R10000 CPUs due to speculative execution. 68 The R10000 does out-of-order execution but retains a branch delay slot. 69 SGI offered upgrades that allowed people to swap the hardware in their cases, such as from an R4400 to an R10000 or from an Express to an IMPACT, while keeping the cases and mechanical parts. 70 An R10000 CPU can be installed on an R5k O2 motherboard sled but requires a taller PCIe cage, a replacement plastic faceplate, and a suitable O2 case. 71 The R10000 bus is supposed to be neat/fast, but it is gimped in the O2 design. 72
