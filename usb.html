USB

Claims: 107 Background: 18 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see USB, Video Graphics Array, Zip drive.

With Firewire

IP35 systems support FireWire mass storage but not USB mass storage, while IRIX supports FireWire storage but not USB storage. 12 IRIX supports OHCI chipsets for USB and FireWire, which is a mostly hardware-based implementation requiring a driver similar to a SCSI card. 3 FireWire performs better than USB in terms of system resource usage and throughput due to DMA transfers and full-duplex capability. 4 The Octane2 does not support USB, but USB support exists in IP35 systems, where USB mice and keyboards can be used before IRIX boots, while FireWire support is present in Octane2 but not built by default. 5 On the 2k, the onboard USB is 1.1 and should use FireWire external disks or a USB2 card for better performance, and USB and FireWire drivers from Fuel/Tezro could potentially work on Octane and O2. 67 A combo FireWire/USB card can be used on SGI Fuel machines, with FireWire detected as DM10 and USB supporting keyboard and mouse. 8 Commercial UNIX vendors neglected FireWire and USB, expecting Macs to fade. 9

General

Late version kernels, starting from 2.2.7, support USB. 10 USB support is assumed to mean storage device support. 11 USB is almost universally regarded as being terrible, and Linux has trouble with USB plug and play. 1213 High-end laptops started getting USB in 98. 14 OHCI is USB 1.1 only, but supports USB Keyboard/Mouse. 15 Some laptops, like Thinkpads, use PS/2 for trackpoints, but trackpads are often USB. 16 The Pismo PowerBook had a FW400 port, built-in Ethernet, and USB; it lacked ADB. 17 The T40 can boot from USB. 18 USB is a compact and durable connector that has evolved many times and supports a wide range of devices with many drivers available. 1920 The PS/2 protocol can be transported over USB using a USB HID interface, and some keyboards support both USB and PS/2. 2122 USB support cannot be added to systems prior to the Fuel due to kernel limitations, and only certain chipsets are supported by IRIX for USB. 2324 On IRIX, USB supports mouse, keyboard, and audio, but only IP35 systems and up support USB, with no mass storage support. 2526 USB can be used to connect to SGI systems via a dongle that creates a /dev entry, though some adapters may be counterfeit. 2728 USB is used for a range of devices and functions, including audio output (e.g., a Griffin iMic with a Fuel), GPIB cards, and reflashing system PROM to rescue machines with corrupted PROM or to flash new PROM images during development. 2930 USB can work seamlessly on old systems with the right drivers, as seen with an IP35 system, though some devices are incompatible with IRIX and most are USB. 3132 USB can be used as a transport for other protocols, which makes them easier to translate, and the same protocol can run over USB transport. 3334 USB can be used for imaging drives, either through the ZULUSCSI (which can display status over USB and sequentially image multiple drives) or by connecting a drive to a computer via USB and using dd_rescue, with a dual Xeon significantly speeding up SCSI drive imaging over USB compared to the Zulu. 353637 USB can conflict with other controllers, such as the onboard USB conflicting with the Adaptec SCSI controller on the Media Bus card, which may require IRQ sharing to work. 38 Some devices are protocol compatible with USB but may not convert to serial, such as Intuos2 tablets. 39
