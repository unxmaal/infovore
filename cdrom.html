CDROM

Claims: 91 Background: 1 claim already covered by Wikipedia, see XFS.

A CD-ROM can be used to boot and install software, either as an ISO image or as a SCSI disk, and the boot command varies based on the drive configuration and SCSI ID. 123 The CD-ROM must be assigned a SCSI ID, with ID 6 recommended for OBP to find it, and non-standard IDs may require manual configuration. 456 Boot issues can arise from a faulty or poorly connected CD-ROM, including problems with the SCSI cable, terminator, or dirty connectors, and a blank screen after installation may indicate the system cannot find the disk. 7891011 Some machines lack internal space for a CD-ROM and require an external SCSI model, while older drives cannot read CD-RWs due to their reflective layer. 1213 The CD-ROM setup is often non-stock and picky with CD-Rs, especially those burnt at high speed, and CD-ROMs from that era may be broken due to leaking capacitors. 1415 Boot commands for SGI machines can target a CD-ROM directly, using paths such as scsi(0)cdrompartitionbootecoff or scsicdrompartitionbootecoff depending on the SCSI channel and partition. 1617 Bootable CD-ROMs must support 512kb/sector, and SCSI emulators such as Zulu's CDROM emulation can be used for SGIs. 181920 Installations of IRIX, A/UX, and ULTRIX can be performed using a CD-ROM, sometimes over a serial console, and ULTRIX installations may require setting the manufacturer/device name to fake the CD-ROM. 21222324 The O2 has a built-in CD-ROM at SCSI ID 4, with a default SCSI ID of four unless physically changed, but it often does not function properly, with issues including a cracked cog on the tray mechanism and a bezel that does not close correctly. 25262728 The CD-ROM can be swapped on a running system by ejecting it, and it may show up multiple times in hinv under different IDs. 2930 Other SGI models vary in CD-ROM support: the IO9 connector above the SCSI port may be for a SCSI backplane and CD-ROM, the 4D/50 does not recognize a CD-ROM through its PROM, and the charcoal reissue included CD-ROM, DVD-ROM, and tape drives. 313233 Tape can restore IRIX installations but is limited to IRIX versions earlier than 6.2 for booting, and XFS changes the on-disk format so the miniroot of the original install CD-ROM cannot read the installed system disk. 3435 Network installation is generally faster than CD-ROM, though netbooting may not always be better, and SCSI termination may need to be disabled. 363738
