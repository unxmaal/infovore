IBM

Claims: 116 Background: 4 claims already covered by Wikipedia, see IBM AIX, CATIA.

With Sun

IBM and Sun were among the companies with internet access before SGI, and both made rackmount computers that fit neatly into racks. 12 Many shops were multi-vendor, using Sun, SGI, IBM, DEC, and HP systems, with compression connectors common on Sun, IBM, and SGI machines. 34 IBM and Sun were involved in exploring VMT, though it was not used in routers. 5

General

IBM developed PowerPC processors as part of the Power processor family, based on the PowerMPC reference platform, with contributions from Motorola, IBM, and Apple. 6 Apple and IBM collaborated on systems to run macOS or AIX, including CHRP/PREP. 7 IBM's OpenPower systems did not use OpenFirmware. 8 AIX is still actively supported and IBM is still producing POWER chips. 9 IBM offers concierge tech support for their servers and allows downloading of OS media without a support contract through a free account. 1011 IBM sells official AIX machines starting at about 900 USD. 12 IBM's business model focused on quality, which contributed to the PCjr's failure due to its high price. 13 Microsoft approached IBM about licensing OS/2 under the Windows name and IBM said no. 14 IBM's Z series uses POWER processors and is more reliable with support contracts. 15 IBM has been promoting the POWER8 as the base for the support base for various reasons. 16 IBM has solid RAID technology with flexible configuration options. 17 IBM used PowerPC CPUs in their RAID controllers and Ethernet NICs. 18 IBM uses microcode extensively. 19 IBM had had massive storage systems like the DS8800 for a while. 20 IBM rivets the fan in place, making it difficult to replace without specialized tools. 21 IBM's systems cannot be updated to reflect the correct serial numbers of two AS/400s, causing issues with IBM service requests and software ordering. 22 IBM continues to support AIX, one of the last two commercial Unix systems still being maintained, alongside Oracle's Solaris, which has seen the decline of most other commercial Unix systems. 2324 AIX can be installed via CD, which IBM made easy, and IBM lost a lawsuit against SCO regarding AIX licensing that established derivative works belong to the developer. 2526 IBM has offloaded some of its products, such as z/VSE, and did not release complete Y2K patches, referring to CSFs instead. 2728 IBM provided chips for the SGI Octane after SGI started a relationship with them in 1995 or 1996, and SGI was close to working with IBM, who were developing graphics chips and promoting their connector technology. 2930 The final Vpro GPUs had IBM markings, and Apple and IBM's PowerPC systems used heatsinks for cooling rather than fans. 3132 IBM used the same plug for the external floppy drive on the PS/2 P70, making it compatible with other drives, and used a non-DB connector for wide SCSI, which was unusual. 3334 The PS/2 interface was adopted by other vendors despite IBM's MCA standard, and IBM had a 486 processor platform for the 70, though this is not it. 3536 The P8 and P9 platforms had extensive IBM documentation, and some IBM servers used Supermicro motherboards. 37
