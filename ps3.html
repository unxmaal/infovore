PS3

Claims: 18 Background: 1 claim already covered by Wikipedia, see MIPS architecture.

Sony took financial risks with the PS3, which uses the Cell processor (a PPC-based chip, switching from the MIPS CPUs used in the PS1 and PS2) that developers found difficult to use effectively. 123 The earliest launch models, CECHA/CECHB (COK-001), used an early EE+GS with external RDRAM as a single IC, while the CECHC (COK-002) had only the GS and emulated the EE on the Cell. 45 In-order execution limited the Xbox 360, PS3, and PPC 970 by 2002. 6 The CEB 1000 was even bigger than the previous machine and had a PC Nvidia card rigged to its bus, and a demo for the PS3 release at 2006 E3 was made on it, running slower than production hardware. 7 Some PS3 consoles can be used to build budget clusters, but Sony updated the firmware to disable OtherOS, limiting this capability, and you can get PS3 stuff working on a retail unit with testkit firmware. 89 Non-cfw PS3 models have different exploits and there has been progress on that front, allowing homebrew and emulators. 10 The PS3 enforces HDCP across the entire console, which is anti-consumer but hard to bypass. 11 Optical pickups fail frequently on PS3 and Xbox consoles, the PS3 can lose video over HDMI but still run over Remote Play (suggesting issues with the GPU or HDMI chip), and it has a brittle PSU cover. 121314
